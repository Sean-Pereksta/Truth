BANNERLANDS — THE SIXTEEN HOUSES

MANUAL RALLY POINTS
Newly trained and paid troops start beside their recruitment building and defend it until you set a rally point.
Click one of your buildings to select it. Shift-click adds or removes buildings. Press R, or click All buildings, to select every owned building.
Right-click land to set a rally for the selected buildings, or click Set rally point and then left-click land. Esc or right-click cancels placement.
Each building keeps its own rally. Selecting all with R and setting a rally also sets the default for buildings constructed later. A local override changes only the selected buildings.
Clear rally makes future recruits defend those buildings again; clearing with all buildings selected also clears the future-building default.
Moving, conquering, expanding, holding, retreating and attacking with your army never change rally points.
Selected buildings show their rally flags and routes. Rally changes affect future recruits; troops already marching keep their current orders.

SELECTED ABILITIES AND THE WAR TABLE
Selecting eligible troops shows their active house power with E, readiness and cooldown. Automatic specialist effects are labeled Automatic; spear braces show Hold H.
The right sidebar keeps the minimap visible below independently scrolling commands. Detailed explanations remain in tooltips, Council house details and the handbook.

CAVALRY CHARGES
Give cavalry a Conquer or Attack order with roughly three clear tiles for a straight run-up. Charges deal impact damage, throw exposed enemies backward and sideways, and carry riders through several ranks while momentum lasts.
Tyrell Thorn Lancers, trained at Garden Stables, hit harder, scatter unbraced enemies farther and retain more momentum through a formation.
Bolton Dread Pikes and Frey Bridge Wardens are dedicated spearmen. Hold them still with H for about half a second, facing the approach, to stop a frontal charge and retaliate. The brace keeps its facing: moving, flanked or rear-attacked spearmen cannot stop the charge. Living braced spears can also stop Thorn Lancers.
Other houses must deny the run-up, use forests and fortifications, focus missile fire, flank the cavalry or surround riders after momentum is spent. Ordinary swordsmen can resist impact but do not copy the spearwall hard stop. Stark held infantry still benefits from its knockback resistance.
Move and Hold orders do not initiate a combat charge. Pull riders out and regroup before charging again. Terrain, collisions and exhausted momentum interrupt the run-up.


500 LEVEL PROGRESSION AND SLOWER INCOME
Weapons, armor and Royal treasury each have 500 upgrade levels for every house.
Weapons add 2% of base damage per level. Armor adds 2% of base troop health per level. These bonuses add together rather than multiplying at every purchase: level 1 gives +2%, level 100 gives +200% (3 times base), and level 500 gives +1,000% (11 times base).
Armor upgrades preserve a wounded soldier's percentage of remaining health. Existing soldiers and newly recruited troops receive the same maximum health; fallen troops do not return through an upgrade.

Royal treasury has diminishing returns: its total income bonus is 12% multiplied by the square root of the level. Level 1 gives +12% income; level 100 gives +120%; level 500 gives about +268.3%, or 3.683 times the income before Treasury. Each extra level adds less income than the one before it.

Upgrade prices increase more steeply with every level. Before house penalties, the next purchase costs 200 + 160 times your current level + 8 times your current level squared.
  Purchase level 1: 200 gold.
  Purchase level 2: 368 gold.
  Purchase level 10: 2,288 gold.
  Purchase level 100: 94,448 gold.
  Purchase level 500: 2,072,048 gold.
House Tully pays 15% more for weapons upgrades. Free Folk armor now reaches level 500 and costs 20% more gold, replacing its old level-1 restriction. The Council shows the current level, the next price, and the current and next total bonuses.

Conquering more land raises income much more slowly. Rich highlands and completed Bannerlands still help, but give diminishing gains instead of a large increase for every Bannerland. An example 49-tile start now earns about 5.28 gold per second instead of 16.4. A sample realm with 5,000 owned tiles earns about 13.1 per second instead of 285, before Treasury upgrades; exact income depends on terrain and completed Bannerlands.
Even owning the whole Continent produces only about 16.8 gold per second before Treasury in the sample realm, or about 62 at Treasury level 500. Starting armies, gold and territory remain equal between houses. Rivals pay the same upgrade prices, use the same income rules, and can progress beyond the former low upgrade limit.

HOUSE TROOPS, MOVEMENT AND CASTLES
Each of the 16 houses has its own specialist troop, production structure, and painted castle appearance. Common troop types remain available, and all houses still start with 70 soldiers, 700 gold and 49 owned tiles.
Every house starts with 280 troop capacity and one castle. Each additional held castle adds 60 capacity: two castles allow 340 troops, three allow 400. Land, completed Bannerlands, production buildings and upgrades do not raise troop capacity; territory still supplies income and structure slots.
Build your house's specialist structure to train its troops automatically and unlock paid specialist companies in Muster. Paid specialist recruitment requires a surviving structure of that exact type. You cannot build or recruit another house's specialists.
Specialist troops use army supply; their buildings use normal structure slots. Displayed company costs and sizes include house bonuses and weaknesses. The automatic company sizes and training times below are base values before house modifiers.
Player and rival AI houses use the same movement, recruitment, specialist effects and castle features. Castle artwork does not add starting health.
Each specialist has its own painted animation sheet, with four walking poses and four attack poses. All artwork is included in the offline game.

AUTOMATIC SPECIALIST EFFECTS
These effects happen automatically when their stated conditions are met. Holding a formation and building a real cavalry charge matter for several specialists; there is no extra ability button to press.
Guard auras protect nearby allies without adding their reductions together; only the strongest applicable guard applies. Repeated slow or poison hits refresh their duration rather than multiplying their strength, and weaker poison cannot replace stronger poison. Shock, snares, slows, poison and temporary fury expire after their listed durations.
Sweeps and piercing shots have a limited number of targets. Their extra hits do not start further sweeps or an endless chain of bolts. River healing is limited for each recipient, so surrounding one soldier with healers cannot bypass its four-second recovery limit.
Dragonfire Acolytes throw fire pots, Dune Skirmishers throw javelins, and Beacon Engineers launch piercing ballista bolts. Fire zones, charged strikes, guard protection and timed conditions also have their own battle cues.
Enemy specialist hits and damaging poison or fire interrupt continuous expansion when they actually damage a member of that expansion party. Simply seeing an enemy does not stop it. After an interruption, select the troops and press T again to resume expansion.
