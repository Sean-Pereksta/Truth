LARGE-ARMY PERFORMANCE

This update reduces late-game work on large maps with fog of war and thousands
of soldiers. It preserves the existing supply rules, combat cadence, formations,
crossbow reloads, sight radii, and knowledge of hidden enemies.

SIMULATION
Soldiers query an ordered local structure index instead of scanning every
building on the continent. Final collision, targeting, and aura checks still
use their original geometry and tie order. Construction, upgrades, casualties,
and replacement arrays cannot leave a stale index between updates.

Cavalry threat checks consider cavalry, and commander auras use the relevant
commanders. Combat uses the current sight grid for live troop positions rather
than repeatedly checking whether the same fog refresh is due. These shortcuts
are scoped to one simulation batch and release even when an update throws.

Factory planning rejects sites that cannot beat the current best score before
performing expensive safety checks. The chosen site and equal-score ordering
remain identical to a full reference search.

FOG OF WAR
Exact circular sight footprints are reused while troops stand still. Moving
sources update the changed perimeter, and overlapping allied sight uses counts
so one departing scout cannot hide ground another scout still sees. Ownership
refreshes scan the world once, then currently visible cells per alliance.

Knowledge queries still inspect live positions, deaths, and newly spawned
soldiers. Hidden building memories remain frozen until rescouting. Cached sight
geometry is released when its source disappears. The extra grids use roughly
7.6 MB on a continent with sixteen separate alliances, plus live source spans.

DRAWING
A normal wide viewport can need more than the previous 24 terrain chunks.
The cache now adapts to the visible working set within a 96 MiB terrain budget,
preventing the same chunks from being evicted and repainted every frame.
Oversized views retain useful cached chunks and draw excess terrain transiently.

Painted troop frames share texture pages. Portraits and all standing, walking,
attacking, facing, and mechanical reload frames retain the same pixels.
House crest grouping remains bounded to ten updates per second as troops enter
or leave the camera; live positions and visibility are checked every frame.

The simulation benchmark supports --counts, --scenes, --frames, --warmup,
--fog on/off, --module for a saved baseline, and --output for JSON results.
Use the realm scene for developed territory with live AI decisions, and battle
for a deliberately dense engagement. The browser benchmark covers 3,000 and
6,000 troops, fog, minimap refreshes, camera movement, and dispersed camps.

LOCAL BEFORE/AFTER MEASUREMENTS
Baseline: main at 4e2128b. Continent, Riverlands, sixteen houses, fog enabled,
256 developed structures, seed 44277; 25 warmup and 75 measured 1/30-second steps.
Mean simulation time per step, milliseconds (lower is better):

                         Before     After
3,000 marching troops      46.9      21.5
3,000 dense battle         54.9      17.4
3,000 developed realm/AI    62.2      26.4
6,000 marching troops     105.2      48.6
6,000 dense battle        194.4      52.2
6,000 developed realm/AI   112.2      47.0

Chrome at 1400 x 900, sixty sampled frames after warmup, using the same current
simulation for both renderers to isolate drawing changes: median drawing time
for the 6,000-troop dense fog scene fell from 107.3 to 27.4 ms (3,437 visible).
The dispersed 6,000-troop scene with 375 visible troops stayed at 16.7 ms median
frame intervals; drawing took 4.4 ms in the new renderer. The full-army overview
still took 66.7 ms median frame intervals, so extremely dense, fully visible
armies remain a demanding case rather than a universal sixty-frame target.

The 6,000-soldier stress fixtures deliberately exceed ordinary starting supply;
the playable game's recruitment and castle capacity rules are unchanged.

Timing depends on hardware, zoom, density, and how many troops are in contact.
Simulation milliseconds and drawing milliseconds are separate measurements;
they should not be presented as a universal frame-rate guarantee.
